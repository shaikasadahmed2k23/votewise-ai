The Indian Election Process

A step-by-step guide to how elections are conducted in the world's largest democracy.

1

Delimitation of Constituencies

Demarcation of boundaries of parliamentary or assembly constituencies to ensure equal representation.

2

Preparation of Electoral Rolls

Updating the voter list (Electoral Roll) to include new eligible voters and remove those who have moved or died.

3

Announcement of Election Schedule

The Election Commission of India (ECI) announces the dates for polling and counting. The Model Code of Conduct comes into effect.

4

Filing of Nominations

Candidates file their nomination papers along with an affidavit detailing their assets, liabilities, and criminal records (if any).

5

Scrutiny of Nominations

The Returning Officer scrutinizes the nomination papers to ensure they are valid and meet all legal requirements.

6

Withdrawal of Candidature

Candidates are given a few days to withdraw their nominations if they change their minds about contesting.

7

Election Campaigning

Political parties and candidates campaign to win voter support. Campaigning officially ends 48 hours before polling begins.

8

Polling Day

Voters cast their votes securely and secretly using Electronic Voting Machines (EVMs) and VVPATs at designated polling stations.

9

Exit Polls

After the final phase of polling concludes, media agencies release exit polls predicting the possible outcome.

10

Counting of Votes

Votes recorded in the EVMs are counted under strict security and observation on a predetermined date.

11

Declaration of Results

The Returning Officer declares the candidate with the highest number of valid votes as the winner of the constituency.
